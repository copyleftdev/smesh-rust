Read A2A

Every claim on the field.
Every window on the record.

Scouts read Kalshi, Massive and Kraken with a client that can only issue GETs to market-data paths. Analysts posed eight hypotheses about Kalshi's prediction markets. A walker tested each on unseen windows; a skeptic doubted small samples and fee-sensitive results.

A claim's strength is its recency times its evidence: the margin of confirming over contradicting windows, shrunk toward zero when there are few. Claims no recent window supports fade.

–

Two limits worth knowing. The calibration test asks whether Kalshi's error is within what chance alone produces; at ~90 predictions a window it can only fail to reject calibration. And Kalshi's payroll ladders missed the prints widely in both directions: check which figure they settle on before leaning on payroll results.

Ask the mesh.
Over A2A, routed by Jev.

The finished study joins the mesh as an agent.
It answers only from what it recorded.

  1. Serve the mesh.

    The study becomes an agent named Quant beside any others, behind one A2A v1.0 endpoint.

  2. Jev routes the question.

    Each agent asks Jev whether it suits the task. A question about Kalshi's odds reaches Quant; a translation does not.

  3. Jev picks the evidence.

    Quant asks Jev which recorded findings bear on the question, then returns them verbatim. It cannot claim more than the walk showed.
